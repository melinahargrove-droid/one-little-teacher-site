A little look. A gentle breeze. A group to remember.

Little play. Growing number sense.

01

Recognize a small group

Practice noticing how many leaves there are without counting each one first—a skill called subitizing.

02

Connect groups to numbers

Choose the numeral that matches the group you saw. Optional dots on the answer cards offer extra visual support.

03

Count and explain

After a correct answer, touch each leaf once to count together. Talk about how you saw the group.

A woodland quick look in three steps

1. Look at the leaves

Tap Ready when everyone is looking. A small group of leaves appears briefly before the whole group blows away.

2. Choose how many

Tap the number of leaves you saw. Need another look? Choose Show me again, or try another number.

3. Count together

The leaves return after a correct answer. Tap each one to hear the count, use Count again, or move to the next round.

You guide the challenge. Choose groups of 1–3, 1–4, or 1–5, a quick look of 1–4 seconds, familiar or varied arrangements, and 1–20 rounds. Dots on answer cards and spoken directions can be switched on or off. Start each new look when the group is ready.
A read-aloud companion

A breezy extension to story time

Pair the game with The Leaf Thief by Alice Hemming, illustrated by Nicola Slater. After reading, invite children to notice leaf groups and describe how they knew how many they saw.

The whole group blows away; this is a quick-look quantity game, not a subtraction exercise.

Before you play

NarrationRecorded directions and counting are embedded. Use Hear to replay the current prompt; spoken directions can be turned off in Teacher’s corner. Directions also appear on screen.
DevicesMouse or touch controls. A tablet in landscape or a larger screen gives the woodland scene more room. Try the controls on your classroom device first.
Offline setupThe game is a self-contained HTML file with artwork and audio included. Save it to your device and open it in a browser. Check sound and offline play before the lesson.
